FHFA Rule Explained

The 25 June 2025 directive: what happened, what it means, and where things stand

What happened on 25 June 2025

FHFA Director Pulte signed a directive ordering Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — the government-sponsored enterprises backing much of the US mortgage market — to consider cryptocurrency as a mortgage reserve asset. The headline detail: borrowers would not need to convert crypto to USD first for it to be considered in reserves.

That last point matters because, historically, crypto holders were often told to liquidate to dollars and season the funds in a bank account before lenders would count them. The directive challenged that default — at least for the reserves portion of an application.

What it means

  • Reserves recognition: crypto holdings can be factored into the reserves assessment on conforming-loan thinking, rather than ignored until converted.
  • No forced conversion: the directive's framing removes the blanket requirement to sell to dollars just to be counted.
  • Signal to lenders: direction of travel is clear — digital assets are being treated as a legitimate part of household balance sheets.

What it doesn't mean

  • Not an automatic approval: lenders still underwrite individually — credit, income, and documentation rules all still apply.
  • Not the down payment: the directive concerns reserves, not the cash delivered at closing. Sellers still expect fiat.
  • Not self-executing: Fannie and Freddie guidance, lender overlays, and verification standards all have to catch up.
  • Not a tax change: pledging vs selling keeps its usual tax character regardless of the directive.

Status as of Sept 2026

Early and unclear: as of mid-2026 public reporting, implementation is still early and unclear — lenders decide individually. Some lenders moved faster than others; many are still working out custody, verification, and volatility-haircut questions. Always confirm with the specific lender (as of Sept 2026 — verify).
