# Why Coinbase's Crypto-Backed Mortgage Is a Big Deal (Even If You Hate Coinbase)

As someone who lives on the Bitcoin Standard, and has as much of his net worth tied to Bitcoin as possible, it is refreshing to see the "Crypto" Giant move into Mortgages. There are many reasons to criticise Coinbase because of its nature for pushing crypto rather than Bitcoin. However, it is still a very big and dominant player in space, especially among institutions.

And that institutional dominance is exactly why this matters more than most people realise.

**What Actually Happened**

Back in March, Coinbase partnered with Better Home & Finance to launch the first crypto-backed conforming mortgage in the US. This week it opened up to eligible Coinbase One members — with Better offering up to $10,000 in closing cost credits for qualifying buyers.

Here's the structure: you don't sell your crypto to buy the house. You take out two loans. The first is a standard Fannie Mae-conforming mortgage. The second is backed by Bitcoin or USDC held in Coinbase custody, and it funds your down payment.

**Why The Institutions Matter**

Fannie Mae has accepted this product. Not a crypto lender. Not a fintech startup. Fannie Mae — the organisation that essentially *is* the US mortgage market.

The largest debt market in the world has just formally acknowledged that Bitcoin is credible collateral. Fannie Mae doesn't do speculative. Fannie Mae does boring, standardised, safe. That stamp matters more than any exchange listing.

**Why This Is Huge On The Bitcoin Standard**

The biggest dilemma of living on the Bitcoin Standard has always been: how do you buy a house without selling your Bitcoin?

For years, the answer was "you don't." You either stayed out of property, or you sold your hardest asset — realising capital gains, losing your position, stepping off the standard — to buy a home.

This product removes that dilemma. Your Bitcoin stays yours, quietly appreciating in custody, while you build equity in a roof over your head. You can own a home *and* hold your sats. That's the whole point.

**The Coinbase Catch**

Now the criticism. Because it is Coinbase, and the catch is always the same one: it's "crypto," not Bitcoin.

The collateral can be Bitcoin *or* USDC. The loan is priced, settled, and denominated in dollars. And Coinbase's real incentive is to keep you inside its ecosystem — including its stablecoin — not to optimise your Bitcoin position.

That's the eternal tension. Coinbase is a giant, and it's dominant with institutions. But it's a crypto company, not a Bitcoin company. It will happily serve you USDC where Bitcoin would serve you better.

**The Fine Print (Read Before You Pledge)**

Now, the honest fine print. This is a loan against your Bitcoin, so let's be clear about the real costs.

First, the good news: there are no margin calls. Your crypto is pledged and locked for the life of the loan — it can't be traded — but if the price falls, nothing changes on the loans, as long as you keep making the monthly payments. That's a fundamentally different (and better) structure than the leveraged lending that has blown people up elsewhere in crypto.

The actual downsides are more boring:

- **Two loans, two lots of interest.** You pay interest on the mortgage *and* the down-payment loan. Better argues its rates offset it, but you're still paying more than a conventional mortgage.
- **Your Bitcoin is locked for the life of the loan.** You can't sell it, can't react to the market, can't put it to work. For someone on the Bitcoin Standard, that's the real price — you're trading flexibility for a home.
- **If you stop paying, the pledged crypto is at risk.** That's standard loan default, not a crypto mechanism. Pay the monthly bill, and a red candle changes nothing.

So the honest take: it's not leverage that can liquidate you overnight. It's a simple, boring bet — keep your Bitcoin, pay extra interest, get a house. For anyone all-in on the standard, that might be the best trade the system has offered yet.

**Bottom Line**

Put the criticism aside, because the signal is bigger than the product. The US mortgage system has accepted Bitcoin as collateral for a home. That's not a meme — that's the system admitting the asset class is here to stay.

The infrastructure keeps arriving. Payments, cards, and now mortgages. Every step makes the same choice easier: you don't have to choose between Bitcoin and the life you want. You can have both.

That's a future worth living on the standard for.
